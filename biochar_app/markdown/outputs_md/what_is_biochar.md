}</style></head><h1 id="what-is-biochar">What Is Biochar?</h1>
<p>Biochar is the solid material produced when biomass is heated in an oxygen-limited environment (<a href="https://biochar-international.org/">International Biochar Initiative</a>). The internal surface area of its pore structure can reach approximately 2,000 square feet per gram, about half the area of a basketball court. Biochar can also hold up to seven times its weight in water (Figure 1).</p>

<h2 id="plant-biology-and-biochar-structure">Plant Biology and Biochar Structure</h2>
<p>For readers interested in the chemistry, a little plant biology is helpful. Most biochar is made from plant material. A key component of vascular plants is lignin, a complex aromatic polymer that contains many carbon-ring structures (the hexagons in Figure 2) and helps stiffen plant cell walls. This structural support was important in the evolution of land plants. Figure 3 shows a simplified model of well-processed biochar. During pyrolysis, much of the easily decomposed material is removed, leaving a carbon-rich structure with many aromatic rings. Microorganisms can readily break down many carbohydrates, but these condensed carbon structures are much more resistant to decomposition. This helps explain why biochar can persist in soil for hundreds to thousands of years.</p>
