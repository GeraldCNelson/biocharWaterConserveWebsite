# Historical preliminary analysis: biochar, microbes, and nutrients, 2023–2025

This analysis was moved from the ignored `biochar_app/results/` directory so
that its observations and supporting tables would not be lost. It is useful as
a starting point for renewed soil-health, nutrient-needs, and fertilizer-effect
analysis.

**Analysis period:** 2023–2025

**Preserved:** 2026-10-17

**Status:** Historical, preliminary research record; not a current published
project conclusion.

See
[`soil_health_results_inventory_2026-10-17.md`](soil_health_results_inventory_2026-10-17.md)
for the file inventory and known limitations.

## Interpretation limits

- The four strips provide descriptive treated-versus-control contrasts, but
  they do not by themselves provide independent replication sufficient for
  strong causal claims.
- The program that generated these particular tables was not found during the
  2026-10-17 repository audit. Input versions, transformations, and assumptions
  therefore need to be reconstructed before the calculations are updated or
  presented as current results.
- Some filenames cover 2023–2025 even though 2025 fall measurements are blank
  in several annual tables.
- Statements about physical retention, biological buffering, or microbial
  mediation are hypotheses suggested by the observed patterns, not mechanisms
  established by this analysis.

The remainder records the original interpretation in more cautious language.

## 1. Microbial Biomass (signal strength)

### Total Biomass (key pattern)

| Year | S1 | S2 | S3 | S4 | Interpretation |
|------|----|----|----|----|----------------|
| 2023 spring | 1844 | 1307 | 1500 | 1565 | S1 exceeds S2; S3 is below S4 |
| 2024 fall | 3332 | 2176 | 2171 | 2114 | Both treated strips slightly or substantially exceed their paired controls |
| 2025 fall | 7345 | 6521 | 5983 | 4913 | Both treated strips exceed their paired controls |

**Preliminary observation:** Microbial biomass was higher in the biochar strip
in the S1/S2 comparison at each listed time and in the S3/S4 comparison in 2024
and 2025. This pattern should be retested using the current canonical data and
an analysis that accounts for sampling and strip-level limitations.

### Bacteria (fast cycling)

- S1/S2 ratio declines over time but remains >1
- S3/S4 spikes strongly in 2025

**Historical interpretation:** The treated-versus-control contrast may indicate
higher bacterial activity, especially later in the period.

### Fungi (structure + P cycling)

- S1/S2 consistently >1
- S3/S4 spikes strongly in 2025

**Historical interpretation:** The contrast may indicate greater fungal
biomass in the treated strips. A connection to phosphorus cycling remains a
hypothesis.

## 2. Plant Tissue Nutrients (what plants accessed)

### Phosphorus (% dry basis)

- Tight range (~0.18–0.36)
- Very small differences between strips

**Preliminary observation:** No strong treated-versus-control difference in
plant phosphorus was apparent in this summary.

### Potassium (% dry basis)

- Larger variation
- Biochar strips generally higher

**Preliminary observation:** The historical analysis identified larger and
more variable potassium differences. Those contrasts should be recalculated
before drawing a treatment conclusion.

---

## 3. System Connections

### K system

- Soil: strong depletion patterns
- Plant: clear biochar advantage
- Microbes: secondary role

**Hypothesis to test:** Physical retention, water availability, or soil
structure could contribute to the observed pattern.

### P system

- Soil: small changes
- Plant: stable
- Microbes: large increase

**Hypothesis to test:** Biological buffering could contribute to the observed
pattern.

---

## 4. Core Insight

The original analysis proposed two possible system responses:

- **K → Physical system**
  - Reduced loss
  - Improved uptake under stress
- **P → Biological system**
  - Stable soil levels
  - Increased microbial mediation

---

## 5. Historical working hypotheses

- Biochar may affect potassium availability through physical retention.
- Biochar may affect phosphorus cycling through changes in microbial biomass.
- Increased microbial biomass may influence the stability of phosphorus
  supply without increasing measured phosphorus concentrations.

---

## 6. Question for renewed analysis

Does biochar increase biological capacity in a way that stabilizes phosphorus
availability, and can that effect be distinguished from strip, year, moisture,
and fertilizer differences?
