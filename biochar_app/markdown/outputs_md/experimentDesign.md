<h2 id="research-questions">Research Questions</h2>
<p>As discussed in the Introduction tab, the process of making biochar leaves massive amounts of space available in the biochar pores where water, nutrients, and soil biota can be stored. In a desert environment like that around Fruita, Colorado, water shortages can be a big problem.</p>
<p>The primary research question for this experiment is how much more water can be stored in a field with biochar than in a field without, and for how long. A second research question is what does biochar contribute to quantity and quality of pasture production. A third question is how long do the benefits of having biochar in the soil last?</p>
<p>The experiment began in Spring 2023 at the Fruita Research Station of Colorado State University. This website covers the period from 2023 through 2026.</p>
<h2 id="experiment-design">Experiment design</h2>
<p>The image below shows the layout of the experimental field. It had previously been an unused plot planted to mixed pasture grasses. The field was divided into four strips, numbered 1 to 4 from left (west) to right (east). Each strip is about 46 ft wide and 280 ft long.</p>
<figure>
<img alt="Experiment layout" src="/static/images/generated/experimentdesign/image-01.webp" style="width:2.15759in;height:3.38598in" title="Experiment layout"/>
<figcaption><p>Figure 1. Experiment layout</p></figcaption>
</figure>
<p>In spring 2023, biochar was injected into strips 1 and 3 every 6 inches to an approximate depth of 6 to 8 inches. Strips 2 and 4 had no biochar amendment. No further biochar applications were made.</p>
<h2 id="irrigation-is-the-source-of-water">Irrigation is the source of water</h2>
<p>Average rainfall in the area is about 9 inches (23 cm) so irrigation is essential. Water for the field comes from Colorado River via the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Highline_Canal">Highline Canal</a>. Water is applied using furrow irrigation, with the furrows 30 inches apart. Irrigation water is applied at the north end (top of the image) and runs down the furrows, infiltrating into the soil (see figure below). Any excess runs into a drainage ditch at the south end of the field.</p>
<p>Irrigation is applied to the east two rows (strips 3 and 4) roughly every two weeks during the growing season. The two west strips are irrigated only once a month.</p>
<h2 id="biochar-source-and-application">Biochar source and application</h2>
<p>The biochar used in this experiment is made from pistachio shells and was purchased from <a href="https://vgridenergy.com/">VGrid Energy Systems</a>, Camarillo, California. Laboratory analyses of the biochar are available in the <a class="tab-link" data-tab="biochar-analysis-tab" href="#">Biochar Material Characterization</a> tab under Lab-Based Results.</p>

<h2 id="irrigation-data-collection">Irrigation data collection</h2>
<p>Data on the consequences of irrigation in soil – soil moisture, electrical conductivity, and temperature – is collected using Campbell Scientific CS650 probes located 6, 12, and 18 inches below the surface. The process is described in detail in the Technical Details tab.</p>

<h2 id="fertilizer-application"><br/>
Fertilizer application</h2>
<p>The Ward Labs fertilizer recommendations based on the soil samples for each strip were followed, with a spring application. The quantity information is available from the Bulk Downloads tab.</p>
<h2 id="yield-quality-and-quantity-measurements">Yield quality and quantity measurements</h2>
<p>Grasses were harvested two to three times a season in each of the four strips with measurements on dried biomass content and analyzed for nutrient composition by strip (<a class="tab-link" data-tab="nir-tab" href="#">Pasture Quality Metrics</a>) and <a class="tab-link" data-tab="biomass-field-tab" href="#">Biomass (Field Samples)</a> tabs).</p>
<p><a class="tab-link" data-tab="soilchem-tab" href="#">Soil Chemistry</a> and Biological Activity</p>
<p>Soil samples were collected in each strip twice a year, in the spring and fall. The data were sent to Ward Labs for testing for both chemical composition and biological activity (Soil <a class="tab-link" data-tab="soilbio-tab" href="#">Biological Health</a> and <a class="tab-link" data-tab="soilchem-tab" href="#">Soil Chemistry</a> tabs). As with all the data presented here, these data can be downloaded using the Bulk Downloads tab.</p>
